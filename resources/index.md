This is a small, growing collection. We explain what each resource can help with and distinguish things we're exploring from our own brewing documentation.

## From the BevOps workbench

### [Brew-day notebook prototype]({{ '/brew-day/' | relative_url }})

A checklist and batch record based on our Punkin’ Monkey runbook. Record actual measurements, keep notes, export or import a batch file, and print the record. Your draft stays in this browser; export it to keep your own copy.

## Calculators we're exploring

### [Brewer's Friend: all-grain OG, FG & ABV](https://www.brewersfriend.com/allgrain-ogfg/)

Estimate original gravity, final gravity, and alcohol from a grain bill. We've started comparing it with our own recipes; we're still learning how its assumptions line up with our brewing setup.

When comparing results, check batch volume, efficiency, fermentable specifications, and yeast attenuation. Keep a note of the inputs so the comparison is useful next time.

### [Brewer's Friend: brewing calculators](https://www.brewersfriend.com/stats/)

A collection covering gravity, dilution, efficiency, carbonation, and more. A useful starting point when you have a specific brew-day calculation in mind.

Two tools worth exploring alongside it:

- [Hydrometer temperature correction](https://www.brewersfriend.com/hydrometer-temp/) — account for sample temperature and your hydrometer's calibration temperature when checking gravity.
- [Refractometer calculator](https://www.brewersfriend.com/refractometer-calculator/) — includes alcohol correction for readings taken after fermentation begins.

**Access note:** Brewer's Friend lists its standalone calculators as free. The site carries advertising and also offers recipe-management memberships with different limits and features. Check its [current membership details](https://www.brewersfriend.com/pricing/) if you want to save and manage recipes.

## A place to start reading

### [How to Brew — John Palmer](https://howtobrew.com/)

An introduction to ingredients, brewing processes, and troubleshooting, with paths into extract and all-grain brewing. Start with the ingredients and process for your brewing method, then follow the questions that come up along the way.

The author identifies the online text as an updated version of the third edition; the published book has a later edition. The website explains the differences.
