## From the BevOps workbench

### [Brew-day notebook prototype]({{ '/brew-day/' | relative_url }})

## Calculators we're exploring

### [Brewer's Friend: all-grain OG, FG & ABV](https://www.brewersfriend.com/allgrain-ogfg/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" title="Opens in a new tab"}

Estimate original gravity, final gravity, and alcohol from a grain bill. We've started comparing it with our own recipes; we're still learning how its assumptions line up with our brewing setup.

When comparing results, check batch volume, efficiency, fermentable specifications, and yeast attenuation. Keep a note of the inputs so the comparison is useful next time.

### [Brewer's Friend: brewing calculators](https://www.brewersfriend.com/stats/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" title="Opens in a new tab"}

A collection covering gravity, dilution, efficiency, carbonation, and more. A useful starting point when you have a specific brew-day calculation in mind.

Two tools worth exploring alongside it:

- [Hydrometer temperature correction](https://www.brewersfriend.com/hydrometer-temp/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" title="Opens in a new tab"} — account for sample temperature and your hydrometer's calibration temperature when checking gravity.
- [Refractometer calculator](https://www.brewersfriend.com/refractometer-calculator/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" title="Opens in a new tab"} — includes alcohol correction for readings taken after fermentation begins.

**Access note:** Brewer's Friend lists its standalone calculators as free. The site carries advertising and also offers recipe-management memberships with different limits and features. Check its [current membership details](https://www.brewersfriend.com/pricing/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" title="Opens in a new tab"} if you want to save and manage recipes.

## A place to start reading

### [How to Brew — John Palmer](https://howtobrew.com/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" title="Opens in a new tab"}

An introduction to ingredients, brewing processes, and troubleshooting, with paths into extract and all-grain brewing. Start with the ingredients and process for your brewing method, then follow the questions that come up along the way.

The author identifies the online text as an updated version of the third edition; the published book has a later edition. The website explains the differences.
